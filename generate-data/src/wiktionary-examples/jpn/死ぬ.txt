<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Etymology">Etymology</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%E6%AD%BB%E3%81%AC&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Etymology"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>From <span class="etyl"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Japanese" class="extiw" title="w:Old Japanese">Old Japanese</a></span>, from <span class="etyl"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Japonic_language" class="extiw" title="w:Proto-Japonic language">Proto-Japonic</a></span> <i class="Latn mention" lang="jpx-pro"><a href="/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Japonic/sin-" title="Reconstruction:Proto-Japonic/sin-">&#42;sin-</a></i>. Appears in the oldest Japanese texts, including the <i><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihon_Shoki" class="extiw" title="w:Nihon Shoki">Nihon Shoki</a></i> of 720 <small class="ce-date"><a href="/wiki/Appendix:Glossary#CE" title="Appendix:Glossary"><span title="Glossary and display preference">CE</span></a></small> and the <i><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man%27y%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB" class="extiw" title="w:Man&#39;yōshū">Man'yōshū</a></i><sup id="cite&#95;ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> of 759 <small class="ce-date"><a href="/wiki/Appendix:Glossary#CE" title="Appendix:Glossary"><span title="Glossary and display preference">CE</span></a></small>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-KDJ&#95;2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KDJ-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-DJR&#95;3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DJR-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-DJS&#95;4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DJS-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="ぬ">ぬ</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>-nu</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> ending historically had an irregular conjugation pattern, shared only by now-obsolete verb <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%BE%80%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="往ぬ"><ruby>往<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</a></span>, <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%8E%BB%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="去ぬ"><ruby>去<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>inu</i></span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss">to <a href="/wiki/go_away" title="go away">go away</a>, to <a href="/wiki/go" title="go">go</a>, to <a href="/wiki/leave" title="leave">leave</a>, to <a href="/wiki/pass" title="pass">pass</a>, to <a href="/wiki/disappear" title="disappear">disappear</a></span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> and auxiliary verb <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="ぬ">ぬ</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>nu</i></span>, <span class="ann-pos">indicating completion, generally reserved for non-intentional, spontaneous, or intransitive actions</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>.  The <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="ぬ">ぬ</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>-nu</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> in verb <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><strong class="selflink"><ruby>死<rp>(</rp><rt>し</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</strong></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>shinu</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> was viewed as identical to the auxiliary,<sup id="cite&#95;ref-DJS&#95;4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DJS-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> and the verb <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><strong class="selflink"><ruby>死<rp>(</rp><rt>し</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</strong></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>shinu</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> initially was thus never conjugated with this auxiliary as an additional suffix.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-KDJ&#95;2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KDJ-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-DJR&#95;3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DJR-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-DJS&#95;4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DJS-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The auxiliary itself may have derived from the verb <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%BE%80%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="往ぬ"><ruby>往<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</a></span>, <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%8E%BB%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="去ぬ"><ruby>去<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>inu</i></span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss">to <a href="/wiki/go_away" title="go away">go away</a>, to <a href="/wiki/go" title="go">go</a>, to <a href="/wiki/leave" title="leave">leave</a>, to <a href="/wiki/pass" title="pass">pass</a>, to <a href="/wiki/disappear" title="disappear">disappear</a></span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>,<sup id="cite&#95;ref-KDJ&#95;2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KDJ-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-DJR&#95;3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DJR-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-DJS&#95;4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DJS-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> raising the possibility that the verb <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><strong class="selflink"><ruby>死<rp>(</rp><rt>し</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</strong></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>shinu</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> may have derived from a fusion of <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E6%AD%BB#Japanese" title="死"><ruby>死<rp>(</rp><rt>し</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby></a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>shi</i></span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss"><a href="/wiki/death" title="death">death</a></span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> + <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%BE%80%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="往ぬ"><ruby>往<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</a></span>, <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%8E%BB%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="去ぬ"><ruby>去<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>inu</i></span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss">to <a href="/wiki/go_away" title="go away">go away</a>, to <a href="/wiki/go" title="go">go</a>, to <a href="/wiki/leave" title="leave">leave</a>, to <a href="/wiki/pass" title="pass">pass</a>, to <a href="/wiki/disappear" title="disappear">disappear</a></span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>; the second element could also be the auxiliary <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="ぬ">ぬ</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>-nu</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> or its suggested ancient stative verb origin.
</p><p>The transition from the <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E3%83%8A%E8%A1%8C%E5%A4%89%E6%A0%BC%E6%B4%BB%E7%94%A8#Japanese" title="ナ行変格活用">ナ<ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>ぎょう</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby><ruby>変<rp>(</rp><rt>へん</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby><ruby>格<rp>(</rp><rt>かく</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby><ruby>活<rp>(</rp><rt>かつ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby><ruby>用<rp>(</rp><rt>よう</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby></a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>na-gyō henkaku katsuyō</i></span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss"><i>na</i>-row irregular conjugation</span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> pattern to the regular <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%9B%9B%E6%AE%B5%E6%B4%BB%E7%94%A8#Japanese" title="四段活用"><ruby>四<rp>(</rp><rt>よ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby><ruby>段<rp>(</rp><rt>だん</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby><ruby>活<rp>(</rp><rt>かつ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby><ruby>用<rp>(</rp><rt>よう</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby></a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>yodan katsuyō</i></span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss"><a href="/wiki/quadrigrade" title="quadrigrade">quadrigrade</a> conjugation</span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> appears in texts from the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muromachi_period" class="extiw" title="w:Muromachi period">Muromachi period</a> (1336–1573) and on.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-KDJ&#95;2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KDJ-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>  The <i>na</i> irregular pattern persisted in increasingly limited use until the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_period" class="extiw" title="w:Meiji period">Meiji period</a> (1868–1929).<sup id="cite&#95;ref-DJR&#95;3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DJR-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>

<div class="mw-heading mw-heading4"><h4 id="Usage_notes">Usage notes</h4><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%E6%AD%BB%E3%81%AC&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Usage notes"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>This Japanese verb denotes an <a href="/wiki/instantaneous" title="instantaneous">instantaneous</a> action.  The Japanese <i>-te / -de</i> + <i>iru</i> grammatical form is usually described in English as equivalent to the <a href="/wiki/present_progressive" title="present progressive">present progressive</a>.  However, for Japanese instantaneous verbs, this <i>-te / -de</i> + <i>iru</i> grammatical form instead more commonly indicates that the action of the verb has completed, and the result of the verb is the new current state.
</p><p>For instance, <i>shinu</i> specifically denotes the <a href="/wiki/instantaneous" title="instantaneous">instantaneous</a> action of dying, whereas the English verb <i class="Latn mention" lang="en"><a href="/wiki/die#English" title="die">die</a></i> denotes more of a process.  To express the idea of <i>someone is now in the process of transitioning from alive to dead</i>, English speakers would use the present continuous construction, <i>someone <b>is dying</b></i>.  However, the similar grammatical construction in Japanese, <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E8%AA%B0%E3%81%8B#Japanese" title="誰か"><ruby>誰<rp>(</rp><rt>だれ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>か</a><a href="/wiki/%E3%81%8C#Japanese" title="が">が</a><b><ruby>死<rp>(</rp><rt>し</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>んで<a href="/wiki/%E3%81%84%E3%82%8B#Japanese" title="いる">いる</a></b></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>dare ka ga <b>shinde iru</b></i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>, instead means that <i>someone <b>is dead</b></i>.  To express the process in Japanese, speakers use the alternative constructions <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><ruby>死<rp>(</rp><rt>し</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>んで<a href="/wiki/%E3%81%84%E3%82%8B#Japanese" title="いる">いる</a><b><a href="/wiki/%E6%89%80#Japanese" title="所">ところ</a><a href="/wiki/%E3%81%A0#Japanese" title="だ">だ</a></b></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>shinde iru <b>tokoro da</b></i></span>, literally <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss"><b>it is the moment of</b> dying</span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> or <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><ruby>死<rp>(</rp><rt>し</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>に<b><a href="/wiki/%E6%8E%9B%E3%81%91%E3%82%8B#Japanese" title="掛ける">かけて</a><a href="/wiki/%E3%81%84%E3%82%8B#Japanese" title="いる">いる</a></b></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>shini<b>kakete iru</b></i></span>, literally <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss"><b>starting</b> to die</span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>.
</p>

<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Trivia">Trivia</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%E6%AD%BB%E3%81%AC&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Trivia"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p><span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><strong class="selflink"><ruby>死<rp>(</rp><rt>し</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</strong></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>shinu</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> is the only modern Japanese verb which ends in <span class="Jpan" lang="ja">〜ぬ</span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>-nu</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> (an older example is <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%BE%80%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="往ぬ"><ruby>往<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</a></span>, <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%8E%BB%E3%81%AC#Japanese" title="去ぬ"><ruby>去<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ぬ</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>inu</i></span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss">to <a href="/wiki/go_away" title="go away">go away</a>, to <a href="/wiki/go" title="go">go</a>, to <a href="/wiki/leave" title="leave">leave</a>, to <a href="/wiki/pass" title="pass">pass</a>, to <a href="/wiki/disappear" title="disappear">disappear</a></span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>, which is now archaic). Accordingly, it is often used in textbooks to illustrate the conjugation pattern for <span class="Jpan" lang="ja">〜ぬ</span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>-nu</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> verbs.
