<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Etymology">Etymology</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%E8%A1%8C%E3%81%8F&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Etymology"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>From <span class="etyl"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Japanese" class="extiw" title="w:Old Japanese">Old Japanese</a></span>, from <span class="etyl"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Japonic_language" class="extiw" title="w:Proto-Japonic language">Proto-Japonic</a></span> <i class="Latn mention" lang="jpx-pro"><a href="/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Japonic/iku" class="mw-redirect" title="Reconstruction:Proto-Japonic/iku">&#42;iku</a></i>. Cognate with <span class="etyl"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_language" class="extiw" title="w:Okinawan language">Okinawan</a></span> <span class="Jpan" lang="ryu"><a href="/w/index.php?title=%E8%A1%8C%E3%81%A1%E3%82%85%E3%82%93&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="行ちゅん (page does not exist)"><ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>ちゅん</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span lang="ryu-Latn" class="tr Latn"><i>ichun</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>, <span class="etyl"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyakoan_language" class="extiw" title="w:Miyakoan language">Miyako</a></span> <i class="Jpan mention" lang="mvi"><a href="/w/index.php?title=%E8%A1%8C%E3%81%8D%E3%81%83&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="行きぃ (page does not exist)">行きぃ</a></i> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span lang="mvi-Latn" class="mention-tr tr Latn">iksï</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>.
</p><p>The original Proto‐Japonic root was vowel‐initial, as evidenced by the Ryukyuan cognates. In Old Japanese, a prothetic glide <span class="IPA">/j/</span> was often inserted before certain vowel‐initial words to ease pronunciation. This phonological innovation created the variant form <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><strong class="selflink"><ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>ゆ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>く</strong></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>yuku</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>, paralleling the <i>i‐</i> ~ <i>yu‐</i> alternation seen in the archaic noun <i class="Jpan mention" lang="ojp"><a href="/wiki/%E5%A4%A2#Old_Japanese" title="夢">夢</a></i> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span lang="ojp-Latn" class="mention-tr tr Latn">ime<sub>2</sub></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> versus modern <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%A4%A2#Japanese" title="夢"><ruby>夢<rp>(</rp><rt>ゆめ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby></a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>yume</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>.
</p><p>Because the innovative <i>yuku</i> form was heavily utilized in foundational classical literature, such as the <i><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man%27y%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB" class="extiw" title="w:Man&#39;yōshū">Man’yōshū</a></i>, it became fossilized as the high‐prestige, conservative literary standard. Meanwhile, the direct descendant <i>iku</i> persisted as the common spoken vernacular and was ultimately adopted as the standard for modern spoken Japanese.
</p>

<div class="mw-heading mw-heading4"><h4 id="Usage_notes">Usage notes</h4><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%E8%A1%8C%E3%81%8F&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Usage notes"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<ul><li>In English, the verbs <i class="Latn mention" lang="en"><a href="/wiki/go#English" title="go">go</a></i> and <i class="Latn mention" lang="en"><a href="/wiki/come#English" title="come">come</a></i> in the senses of physical motion are often used in relation to the listener.  If Alice calls Bob and asks him if he is on his way to her party, he would say, "I'm coming."</li></ul>
<dl><dd>However, in Japanese, the verbs <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><strong class="selflink"><ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>く</strong></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>iku</i></span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss">to <a href="/wiki/go" title="go">go</a></span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> and <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E6%9D%A5%E3%82%8B#Japanese" title="来る"><ruby>来<rp>(</rp><rt>く</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>る</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>kuru</i></span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss">to <a href="/wiki/come" title="come">come</a></span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> are used in relation to the speaker.  If Alice calls Bob and asks him if he is on his way to her party, he would instead say <i class="Jpan mention" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E8%A1%8C%E3%81%8D%E3%81%BE%E3%81%99#Japanese" title="行きます">行きます</a></i> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="mention-tr tr">ikimasu</span>, <span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">“</span><span class="mention-gloss">I'm going</span><span class="mention-gloss-double-quote">”</span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>.</dd></dl>
<ul><li>This verb has an irregular <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onbin" class="extiw" title="w:Onbin">euphonic stem</a> in its perfective and conjunctive constructions, those being <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E8%A1%8C%E3%81%A3%E3%81%9F#Japanese" title="行った"><ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>った</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>itta</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> and <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/w/index.php?title=%E8%A1%8C%E3%81%A3%E3%81%A6&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="行って (page does not exist)"><ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>って</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>itte</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> respectively, instead of the expected <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>いた</span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>iita</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> and <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>い</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>いて</span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>iite</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> or <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>ゆ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>いた</span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>yuita</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> and <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><ruby>行<rp>(</rp><rt>ゆ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>いて</span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>yuite</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>.</li>
<li>As an auxiliary, it is more commonly spelled in hiragana. The form <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E3%81%A6#Japanese" title="て">て</a>いく</span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>-te iku</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span> can also be colloquially shortened to <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E3%81%A6%E3%81%8F#Japanese" title="てく">てく</a></span> <span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">(</span><span class="tr"><i>-teku</i></span><span class="mention-gloss-paren annotation-paren">)</span>.</li>
<li>While generally written as <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E8%A1%8C#Japanese" title="行">行</a></span>, this verb can be written with several other kanji to convey specific nuances. For instance, <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%BE%80#Japanese" title="往">往</a></span> emphasizes proceeding towards a destination or the passage of time, while <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E9%80%9D#Japanese" title="逝">逝</a></span> is a euphemism meaning ‘to pass away’. Other rare, historical, or literary spellings include <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E9%81%A9#Japanese" title="適">適</a></span>, <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E4%B9%8B#Japanese" title="之">之</a></span>, and <span class="Jpan" lang="ja"><a href="/wiki/%E5%BE%81#Japanese" title="征">征</a></span> (the latter typically implying a military campaign or expedition).</li></ul>
