<div class="mw-heading mw-heading4"><h4 id="Usage_notes">Usage notes</h4><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=fazer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=25" title="Edit section: Usage notes"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<ul><li>When related to weather or the passage of time, the verb <i class="Latn mention" lang="pt"><strong class="selflink">fazer</strong></i> is impersonal in standard usage, and therefore cannot take a subject. It is also not inflected for <i>number</i> or <i>person</i>: it is used in the singular third-person form:</li></ul>
<dl><dd><dl><dd><span class="h-usage-example"><i class="Latn mention e-example" lang="pt">Faz duas horas.</i> ― <span class="e-translation">Two hours have passed.</span></span></dd>
<dd><span class="h-usage-example"><i class="Latn mention e-example" lang="pt">Faz três segundos.</i> ― <span class="e-translation">Three seconds have passed.</span></span></dd>
<dd><span class="h-usage-example"><i class="Latn mention e-example" lang="pt">Faz cinquenta anos.</i> ― <span class="e-translation">Fifty years have passed.</span></span></dd></dl></dd></dl>
<dl><dd>If not impersonal, it is conjugated normally. This happens because the third-person neuter singular is normally only implied (for demonstrative pronouns, see <i class="Latn mention" lang="pt"><a href="/wiki/isto#Portuguese" title="isto">isto</a></i> and <i class="Latn mention" lang="pt"><a href="/wiki/aquilo#Portuguese" title="aquilo">aquilo</a></i>).</dd></dl>
